## Why these licences

- **Permissive code (MIT OR Apache-2.0).** A time standard only works if its arithmetic can go everywhere: phone apps, watch firmware, operating systems, servers — including closed products. Apache-2.0 adds an explicit patent licence with retaliation; MIT keeps the code usable in GPLv2-only projects such as the Linux kernel.
- **CC BY for the Standard.** Standards bodies, textbook authors and the press need to republish and adapt the text freely. The meaning that matters — 10 / 100 / 1,000 / 100,000, the kè — is arithmetic, which copyright cannot hold anyway. Integrity of the standard is protected by the name: only Nu-Time Inc.'s text is "the Nu:Time Standard".
- **Permissive hardware (CERN-OHL-P).** The reference clock exists so manufacturers can turn it into products quickly.
- **Control through the trademark.** Like Bluetooth or USB, ubiquity comes from open terms and quality comes from the name and the "Nu:Time Certified" programme.
