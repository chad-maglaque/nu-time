# Back-translation drift check: Spanish (`locales/es.json`)

Method: every string in `es.json` was back-translated into literal English first, then compared with `en.json` string by string. Keys, array lengths and digit sequences were also checked programmatically. They all match, apart from two harmless differences. `heritage.items[0].era` uses "s. XVII" for "17th c.". `why.items[1].text` reorders the clause "desde 1972 ha tenido que añadir 27", which keeps the same figures.

## Overall verdict

The Spanish translation is faithful and reads naturally in neutral international Spanish. Every claim, number, date, unit and licence name matches the English. The leap-second wording "a más tardar en 2035 dejarán de añadirse" is accurate and no stronger than the source. Brand strings (`Nu:Time`, `Nu:Time™`, `Ke-Shi`, `刻时`) are unchanged, and the glossary units are used consistently: gong/gongs, kè (invariable), beat/beats and tic/tics. `gong:beat:tic` is applied correctly in prose, and "huso horario", "esfera" and "ubicación" are each used consistently. The jokes keep their intent: "todo eso que nunca acabaste de entender", "se acabaron los dos martes seguidos", and "Por eso sabemos que ha llegado la hora". The Spain heritage card is factual and not mocking, as the brief requires. The China standards card ("se ofrece como estándar abierto a ese trabajo") keeps the "offered to, not endorsed by" framing. The four flags are all low-severity: one slightly off idiom, one vaguer date preposition shared with the Italian, one narrowed sense of "free", and one optional exonym choice. One more note, not counted as drift: the role labels use the masculine generic (Desarrollador, Investigador, Inversor, Curioso), which is standard Spanish. The Italian page uses activity nouns instead, and the team can switch to those if it wants gender-neutral labels.
