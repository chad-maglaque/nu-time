# Back-translation drift check — French (`locales/fr.json`)

Source: `locales/en.json`. Every string in `fr.json` was back-translated into literal English and compared with the source. The table lists only real drift. Acceptable localisation is not listed: number formats (`1 000`, `16,67`, `3,456 MHz`), French typography, local exonyms (Pékin, Le Caire, Mexico), the `tic` unit name, gender-neutral role labels, and idioms that keep the intent ("avait simplement un temps d'avance", "l'heure est venue").

## Overall verdict

The French translation is faithful. No string changes a number, date, unit, brand name or licence. The structure and claims of the technical sections (units, kè, geometry and trigonometry tables, quartz table, licences, roadmap) match the source exactly. Sensitive content is preserved at the source's strength: the China standards card ("proposé pour ce chantier" keeps Nu:Time offered *to* that work, not endorsed by it), the Spain card, and the leap-second and DST facts. The jokes and puns land in French. "Avait simplement un temps d'avance" and "Voilà comment nous savons que l'heure est venue" keep the intent of the English. The seven flags are all low severity: small shifts in emphasis (one claim made stronger, two made weaker), one added qualifier in the "eliminates" joke, a synonym inconsistency for the Prime Meridian, and an exonym choice. None needs to block release. The `build.lead` and `why.items[1].title` fixes are the most worthwhile.
