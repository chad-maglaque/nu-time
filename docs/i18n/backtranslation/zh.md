# Back-translation drift check — Simplified Chinese (`locales/zh.json`)

Source: `locales/en.json`. Every string in `zh.json` was back-translated into literal English and then compared with the source. The first table lists only real drift. The second table lists the deliberate mainland-audience softenings, which are not errors. Acceptable localisation is not listed. That covers number formats (`1000`, `10 万`, `86400`), full-width punctuation, the 刻时 Nu:Time brand pairing, 更/刻/分/秒 unit names, Chinese exonyms (台北, 香港 and the other cities), and idioms that keep the intent ("给时间做减法", "总把人绕晕", "十进制大革命", "地基都要换了，门面也可以跟着换", "时候到了").

Terminology check: the terms are consistent throughout. 更/刻/分/秒 are used everywhere. Where 分/秒 could be read as ordinary minutes or seconds, the page writes 刻时分 / 刻时秒 (`hero.dialHint`, `build.quartzTitle`, `build.colCycles`). "universal Nu:Time" is always 统一刻时 (`hero.clockLabel`, `hero.dayBar`), and "universal" elsewhere is always 统一 / 全球统一. The brand is paired as 刻时 Nu:Time at the first mention in each section. All numbers, dates and units match the source: 1793–1795, 1916, 1940, 1972, 27 leap seconds, 2019, about 2030, 2035, 86400, the quartz table and the geometry and trig tables.

## Overall verdict

The Chinese translation is faithful and well localised. No number, date, unit, licence, trademark statement or technical claim is changed. The unit system is handled carefully: 更/刻/分/秒 are consistent, 刻时分/刻时秒 remove the minute/second ambiguity where it matters, and 统一刻时 is used consistently for "universal Nu:Time". The heritage cards keep the English's historical claims at the same strength, and the jokes and idioms land naturally. The six flagged items are small shifts in emphasis. One is medium: the leap-second card title is weakened from "the problem" to "a nuisance". The rest are low: a few bold claims are toned down, one roadmap line is stronger, and 秒 is ambiguous in the roadmap line. None blocks release. The China-related content deliberately differs from the English in the two places the brief anticipates. The time-zones card becomes neutral geography with Beijing Time presented as a unified national time. The standards card uses China's own policy language ("actively participating in and promoting"), calls BeiDou and NTSC "important forces" rather than "pillars", and offers Nu:Time as a reference to this work rather than as its standard. The company should know that the zh page makes noticeably weaker claims about China's leadership role, and no 2035 date claim, compared with the English.
