# es — translator notes

- **Tú, not usted.** Tú is the default for tech brands in Spain and Latin America alike (Apple, Google, Spotify), and it fits the confident, warm brief; usted would sound like a bank. No vosotros/ustedes forms anywhere, and no voseo. Where English addresses the reader in the plural, I rephrased to singular or impersonal.
- **Neutral vocabulary.** I avoided markers of one region: "ubicación" (not localización), "correo electrónico", "Escribe un correo válido" (not *Introduce* / *Ingresa*), "Algo falló" / "No se pudo" (not *No se ha podido*). For "time zone" I used **huso horario**, which RAE prefers and everyone understands, even though *zona horaria* is more common in Latin America.
- **Units** follow the glossary: gong/gongs, kè (unchanged in the plural), beat/beats, **tic/tics**. Beat stays in English. Spanish readers will take it as a loanword (as in music), but it is an anglicism. If it reads oddly in testing, the natural fallback would be *pulso*.
- **Leap second = "segundo intercalar"** (the metrology and Fundéu term) rather than the popular *segundo bisiesto*. "Daylight saving" = **horario de verano**, and the act of changing clocks = *cambio de hora*. "A billion people" = **mil millones** (never *un billón*, which means 10¹² in Spanish).
- **Spain heritage card.** I kept it strictly factual: most of Spain lies west of Greenwich, CET was adopted in 1940, and clocks run an hour or more ahead of the sun. The title is "Un país fuera de su huso horario" (out of its *geographic* time zone), not "equivocado" (wrong), which reads as a judgement to a Spanish reader. "What time zones do to daily life" became "cómo los husos horarios **condicionan** la vida cotidiana", which is neutral. I did not mention Franco or Germany, because the English doesn't and it would politicise the card. The *why* card says "España vive con una hora entera de desfase".
- **FLAG: `ke.facts[2..3].n` = "Longitude" / "Latitude" are left in English.** The checker treats every `n` field as must-be-unchanged, but these render as visible card headlines (build.mjs:142). Spanish should be **Longitud / Latitud**. Recommend exempting these two from the verbatim rule, or moving the labels out of `n`.
- **FLAG: `how.cards[].n` = "1,000" / "100,000"** must also stay verbatim and render as big numbers. In Spain (and much of South America) a comma is the decimal mark, so "1,000" reads as *one*. Spanish should be **1000 / 100 000**. The same applies to the decimal points in the geometry and quartz tables ("16.67 kè", "28,311.552"). I kept them per the brief, but Spanish readers from comma-decimal countries will stumble. In running text I followed RAE: 1000 with no separator, 100 000 with a non-breaking space.
- **FLAG: "gong:beat:tick" in `how.lead`** is kept verbatim because the brief lists that pattern as untouchable. Everywhere else the unit is *tic*, so the Spanish page shows "tick" once. If it is not a machine-readable label, "gong:beat:tic" would be better.
- **Trig notation:** I used **sen** (the standard Spanish school notation) in the trig table header and in `geometry.trig`, instead of *sin*. "cos" and "tan" are unchanged. Revert to "sin" if the table must match calculator keys.
- **"Estándar" vs "norma":** I chose *el Estándar Nu:Time* (capitalised as a proper name). *Norma* suggests ISO/UNE paperwork, and the standards-body sense is covered by "organismos de normalización".
- **"Nu:Time Certified"** is left in English as a certification mark name, like the trademarks. Translate it as *Certificado Nu:Time* if it is not meant to be a mark.
- **AM/PM** became "a. m. y p. m." (RAE spelling). Spain mostly uses the 24-hour clock, but the joke still lands because Latin America uses a. m./p. m. daily.
- **Gender:** I used the generic masculine in the role list ("Desarrollador", "Investigador / académico", "Curioso, sin más"), because slashes are already separators there and "/a" forms would clutter it. Where possible I used neutral phrasing instead: "Te damos la bienvenida", "¡Pruébalo por tu cuenta!", "Podré darme de baja".
- **English that didn't carry over:** "It's a finished one" became "Ahora está completa" (*acabada/terminada* would sound like "it's over"). "Short enough for a meeting" became "lo bastante breve para fijar una reunión". The pun "It's time" became "Ya es hora", and "That's how we know the time is now" became "Por eso sabemos que ha llegado la hora". `time.lead` repeats itself in English ("…it doesn't change the time"), so I dropped the repetition. "Kè" was capitalised mid-sentence in `how.cards[3]`, so I normalised it to lowercase.
- **Cities** use Fundéu-recommended exonyms: Pekín, Bombay, Reikiavik, Riad, Yakarta, Shanghái, Taipéi, Sídney, Dubái, São Paulo, Washington D. C. *Pekín* and *Bombay* are standard in Spain and accepted in Latin America, though some Latin American media use *Beijing* and *Mumbai*.
