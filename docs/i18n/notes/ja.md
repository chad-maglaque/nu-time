# ja (日本語) — translator notes

- **Superseded in review:** the kè is written in Latin script, "100 kè" (ケー reads as the letter K, and "100K" means 100,000 in Japanese tech slang). Units are ゴング・kè・ビート・ティック; the unit cards add pinyin (分 fēn, 秒 miǎo) so the characters are not read as minute/second; solar noon is 正中時刻. See `reviews/ja.md`.
- **Units: katakana ゴング・ケー・ビート・ティック (one system, no plurals).** This is the key decision. Kanji was rejected for three reasons. (1) 分 and 秒 always read as SI minutes and seconds in Japanese, and Japanese has no natural equivalent of the Chinese 刻时分/刻时秒 qualifier. The live readout `05:37:50` labelled 更/分/秒 would be read as "37 minutes 50 seconds", when 1 beat = 86.4 s and 1 tick = 0.864 s. (2) 刻 already has a Japanese time meaning: the Edo-period 一刻 was about 2 hours (辰の刻, 半刻), not 14.4 minutes. (3) 更 (こう) is archaic in Japanese, known only from 初更/五更. Katakana matches how Japanese tech readers meet new units, mirrors the English names, and leaves the kanji free for the history.
- **The kanji link is kept where the English shows it.** The unit cards read ゴング · 更, ビート · 分, ティック · 秒, ケー · 刻, exactly like the English "gongs · 更". The China heritage card says Nu:Timeの中心にあるケーは、まさにこの刻です. The licence line glosses each name (ゴング（gong、更 gēng）…). **Reviewer check:** "ビート · 分" and "ティック · 秒" on the cards could briefly suggest minute/second. If that looks risky, add pinyin (分 fēn, 秒 miǎo) or drop the Chinese characters from those two cards.
- **ケー (kè).** Follows the Latin spelling, as in other languages (ru кэ). Its weakness is that ケー also reads as the letter K. The alternatives were worse: コク is the Japanese reading of 刻, but it also means "richness" (of flavour), and Latin "kè" breaks the katakana system. Written without a space after digits (100ケー, 16.67ケー).
- **Brand.** Nu:Time is never localised. 刻时 Ke-Shi is kept exactly, with the simplified 时, because it is a fixed token. A Japanese reader would expect 刻時, so this should be confirmed as intended. "The Nu:Time Standard" → Nu:Time標準. "open standard" → オープン標準. 標準 is used throughout, including 標準仕様 (nav and links) and 標準化団体, rather than mixing it with 規格 or スタンダード.
- **"universal Nu:Time" → 世界共通Nu:Time / 世界共通の時刻.** This avoids 世界時, which means UT. The clock label is 現在の世界共通Nu:Time.
- **Register.** Prose uses です・ます. Headings are short plain or noun-ending (体言止め) lines, for example 時間と場所を、描きなおす。 and なぜ、いまなのか。 The text avoids あなた except where the English leans on "your" (あなたの頭上の太陽, あなたの位置). The form uses polite UI wording (お名前, 送信しています…).
- **"time and location reimagined" → 時間と場所を、描きなおす。** 再定義 was avoided, because it is the launch-event cliché the zh review warned about. 描きなおす keeps "imagine". time.title uses 時間を、とらえなおす。 so it doesn't repeat the hero.
- **"Nu:Time eliminates."** The heading is Nu:Timeがなくすもの。, and the list follows as そもそもよく分からなかった、こんなものたち。… The base-60 items are 1時間60分、1分60秒, so it is clear what goes. Daylight Saving → サマータイム, the everyday Japanese word.
- **Solar noon → 南中時刻.** This is the familiar school term. Strictly, the sun culminates in the north in the southern hemisphere. 正中時刻 is the precise alternative if a reviewer prefers it.
- **Roles** use activity or field labels (ソフトウェア開発, 研究・学術, 標準化・行政…) under 立場, per the brief.
- **Numbers.** 1,000 grouping, decimal point, and 10万 in prose (10万ティック). The card `n` and all table and quartz values keep 1,000 / 100,000 / 2,985,984 unchanged. 86,400 stays as digits. Ranges use 〜 (0〜50ケー, 1793〜1795年).
- **Proper names.** 北斗 → 衛星測位システム「北斗」（BeiDou）. The National Time Service Center is given its native kanji name, 中国科学院国家授時中心. That is normal in Japanese, unlike the it/de decision to keep the English name. "光原子時計" was used, with no mention of the Japanese optical-lattice clock (no added Japan content).
- **English that didn't map neatly.** "Your place in the sun" → あなたの頭上の太陽 (the idiom doesn't exist in Japanese). "That's how we know the time is now" → いまがその時だと言える理由は、ここにあります. "Open. Build one." → オープン。だから、つくれる。 "Nu:Time Certified" stays in English as a mark name, as in fr.
- **Footer "Privacy" → プライバシーポリシー**, the standard Japanese footer label. It is also the title of docs/i18n/privacy/ja.md.
- **Cities.** Standard Japanese forms: 北京, 上海, 香港, 台北 and 東京 in kanji, the rest in katakana. ニューヨーク (no "City"), ワシントンD.C., マドリード, ヨハネスブルグ, レイキャビク.
