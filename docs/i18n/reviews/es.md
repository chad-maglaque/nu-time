# Spanish (es) review

Independent fresh-eyes review of `locales/es.json` against `locales/en.json` and `docs/i18n/BRIEF.md`. Translator notes were not read. Overall the translation is accurate, natural and consistently uses tú; the Spain heritage card is factual and respectful. 31 changes, mostly number formats, labels left in English, and a handful of wording fixes.

Conventions confirmed and kept: trig labels **sen**/cos/tan (Spanish school notation, used in both `geometry.trigCols` and `geometry.trig`); thousands separator is a non-breaking space for 5+ digits and none for 4 digits (RAE), decimal comma (brief); unit names gong / kè / beat / tic, plurals gongs / kè / beats / tics; city exonyms follow Fundéu.

## Needs a human decision

- **Decimal separator for Latin America.** The brief mandates a decimal comma (16,67), which is right for Spain and most of South America, but Mexico, Central America, much of the Caribbean and Peru use a decimal point, and the RAE's *Ortografía* (2010) actually recommends the point. With the comma, "3,456 MHz" could be misread as 3456 MHz by a Mexican engineer. Applied the brief; flagging in case one market matters more.
- **Masculine generic in the sign-up roles** ("Desarrollador", "Investigador / académico", "Inversor / socio", "Curioso, sin más"). Standard and RAE-approved, but some audiences (especially tech/creator communities) expect inclusive forms ("Desarrollador/a") or field names ("Desarrollo de software"). Left as is; a product decision.
- **"Bombay" vs "Mumbai".** Fundéu still recommends Bombay, but much Latin American and some Spanish press now writes Mumbai. Kept Bombay. Likewise "Pekín" (Fundéu) vs "Beijing" (common in Latin America).
- **"Estándar" with a capital E** when it means the named document ("El Estándar Nu:Time", nav "El Estándar"). Spanish would normally lowercase a common noun; capitalising works only if it is treated as the document's proper name. Kept for consistency with the English branding.
- **Anglicisms kept on purpose:** "beat" (glossary), "maker", "influencer". All are current in Spanish tech usage, but a human may prefer "creador/a de contenido" alone.
- **`why.items[5].title`**: the English "being re-written in 2035" is odd; the Spanish says "de cara a 2035" (looking toward 2035), which is the sensible reading. Confirm that is the intended meaning.
- **Quotation marks**: `build.cert` uses «» (RAE-preferred, standard in Spain); Latin American sites often use "". Either is correct.
